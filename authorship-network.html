Collaboration

The two graphs on this page

G_fac, the faculty graph
One node for each of our publishing faculty at IISER Pune and IISER Kolkata. Two faculty are linked if they wrote at least one paper together in 2016–26.
G_ind, the Indian graph
G_fac plus every co-author on our papers whose affiliation is an Indian institution other than the two IISERs, for example CSIR-NCL, TIFR or Savitribai Phule Pune University. That adds people. Our own students and postdocs are not in it, and neither are foreign co-authors.
Groups
Found with the Leiden algorithm. It splits a graph into groups whose members have many more links among themselves than the same number of links placed at random would give. Groups are not the same as connected pieces of the graph: one connected cluster can split into several groups if it has dense cores joined by a few links.

Papers with 100 or more authors are left out of both graphs (see Big collaborations). A link is stronger when two people share more papers and when those papers have fewer authors.

What are the co-author groups made of?

How groups are found
  • Leiden maximises modularity: the share of link strength that falls inside groups, minus the share expected if the same links were placed at random. G_fac's best split scores on a scale up to 1, which means the groups are clear-cut.
  • Faculty with no faculty co-author are left out; Leiden would make each of them a group of one.
  • Louvain, an older algorithm with the same goal, gives almost the same groups (agreement score , where 1 means identical).

Who connects the groups?

How betweenness is computed

Shortest chains use distance 1/strength, so two people who share many small-team papers are "close". Values are normalised by the number of pairs, so they lie between 0 and 1. In G_ind, the outside co-authors also count as possible bridges.

What changes when we add Indian co-authors?

Groups with three or more of our faculty.

G_ind is built outward from our faculty. An outside co-author appears only through one of our papers, so every link between two outside people comes from a paper with one of our faculty on it. The graph therefore shows our faculty's Indian neighbourhood, not Indian science as a whole.
