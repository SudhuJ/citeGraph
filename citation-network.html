How self-citations are identified

A citation is a self-citation when the two papers share at least one author. That covers our faculty and also their students and postdocs. All OpenAlex profiles of a faculty member count as the same person, since OpenAlex sometimes splits one person into several profiles.

What PageRank is, and why not the clustering coefficient
  • PageRank imagines a reader who starts at a random paper and keeps following a random reference, jumping to a new random paper 15% of the time. A paper's PageRank is the share of time the reader spends on it. A citation from a well-cited paper therefore counts for more than one from an uncited paper. The assignment asks for it, so we report its rank beside the plain count.
  • Across the papers cited at least once by another group, the two rankings have a rank correlation of (1 would be the same order).
  • The clustering coefficient answers a different question: whether a paper's neighbours also cite each other, that is, how tightly knit its neighbourhood is. It does not measure how much a paper is used, so it cannot rank importance.
  • For overall influence, the total citation counts on Output & venues are the better measure; this section is only about use inside the two institutes.
